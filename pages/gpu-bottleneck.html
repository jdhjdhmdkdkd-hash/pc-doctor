What Is a GPU Bottleneck?

A GPU bottleneck happens when the graphics processor becomes the main limiting component for performance in a particular game or application.

What Does a GPU Do?

The GPU, or Graphics Processing Unit, is responsible for rendering graphics and performing many visual calculations. Modern games can place a significant workload on the GPU, especially at high resolutions and graphics settings.

Example of a GPU Bottleneck

Suppose you are playing a game at high graphics settings and the GPU is consistently working close to its maximum capacity. In this situation, GPU performance may limit the achievable frame rate.

How to Identify a GPU Bottleneck

  • Monitor GPU usage while playing a game.
  • Monitor CPU usage at the same time.
  • Check your FPS and frame-time behavior.
  • Try lowering graphics settings.
  • Compare performance at different resolutions.

Does a GPU Bottleneck Mean Something Is Wrong?

Not necessarily. Every computer system has a performance limit somewhere. Depending on the game, resolution and graphics settings, the GPU or CPU may become the limiting component.

GPU Bottleneck vs CPU Bottleneck

A GPU bottleneck generally means the graphics processor is limiting performance. A CPU bottleneck means the processor is limiting performance instead.

The limiting component can change depending on the game, graphics settings and workload.
